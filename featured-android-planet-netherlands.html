We Made Android Planet's Best Apps of the Week
in the Netherlands

By John Arthur · Last updated August 25, 2026

Last week, Valen showed up in Android Planet's Best Apps of the Week roundup for the Netherlands. No ad budget, no PR agency, no launch campaign. Just a solo-built recovery app getting picked up by one of the country's largest independent Android publications because it did something the roundup hadn't seen before.

If you're reading this because you clicked through from that feature, welcome. Here's the honest version of what happened and what Valen actually is, no marketing gloss.

What Android Planet actually is

Android Planet is one of the biggest Dutch-language Android publications, and every week their editors round up the apps and games they think are worth Android users' time. It's not a paid placement and it's not an award with a trophy. It's a group of people who cover Android professionally looking at a lot of apps and deciding which ones are actually good.

Getting into that list once doesn't make Valen a finished product or a proven business. It means one week, a handful of editors who look at Android apps for a living thought this one was worth telling people about. That's a real signal, and we're not going to inflate it into more than it is.

What got noticed

Most fitness and workout-logging apps treat "chest" or "shoulders" as a single number. You trained chest, chest is now some percentage recovered, done. Valen doesn't do that. It breaks every major muscle into its functional subzones, upper vs. mid vs. lower chest, anterior vs. posterior deltoid, long vs. short head of the biceps, and tracks fatigue separately for each one.

In practice that looks like this: a real Valen user's chest can show mid chest at 50% recovered while lower chest sits at 18% recovered, on the same day, after the same training week. A whole-muscle app would have averaged that into one meaningless "chest: 34%" number. Valen shows you the actual split, because that split is usually where the useful information was hiding the whole time.

No wearable, no heart-rate sensor, no subscription required to see it. It's calculated entirely from what you log, and the core heatmap is free.

The honest part

Valen is built by one person, not a company, not a corporation with a fitness division, not a VC-backed team with a growth budget. That's not a sympathy pitch, it's just context for what you're looking at. It means updates ship when they're ready rather than on a quarterly roadmap, support emails get answered by the person who wrote the code, and a press feature like this one gets read directly, there's no marketing department it gets routed to first.

It also means Valen isn't finished. The muscle-subzone data is real and it's the reason apps keep getting recommended in fitness communities and now in press roundups, but there's a long list of things still being built, including bringing the app to iOS.

If you're trying Valen for the first time

The free tier is not a trial. The recovery heatmap, unlimited workout logging, the subzone-mapped exercise library, daily training suggestions, and 30 days of history are all free, permanently, no account required. Pro ($24.99/year or $49.99 lifetime) adds unlimited history, the adaptive weekly planner, and deeper subzone insights, if you end up wanting more than the free tier gives you.

Log a few sessions, open the heatmap, and see whether one side of a muscle you assumed was "recovered" actually isn't. That's usually the moment it clicks.
