Hevy vs Strong vs Valen:
The Honest Comparison for Gym Bros

By John Arthur · Last updated August 22, 2026

Three apps walked into a gym. Two of them logged the workout. One of them actually knew if you should be there.

This is a feature by feature breakdown of Hevy, Strong, and Valen. No fluff, no sponsored opinions. Just what each app does and where it quietly gives up.

Workout Logging

All three let you log sets, reps, and weight. This is table stakes at this point.

Strong is the fastest. The interface was built around one thing and one thing only — getting sets logged between rest periods without fumbling around. It's genuinely hard to beat on pure logging speed.

Hevy is close behind. Slightly more visual, slightly more modern. Logging feels good. Not quite as snappy as Strong but most people wouldn't notice the difference.

Valen logs workouts fine. Exercise library, custom exercises, sets and reps, RPE, session notes, rest timer built in. Where it differs is that every exercise you log feeds the recovery model automatically. So logging isn't just record-keeping, it's input data. Your chest day isn't just a workout entry, it's telling the app exactly what stress you put on upper chest, mid chest, lower chest, anterior delts, and triceps separately.

Winner: Strong on raw speed. Valen on what the log actually does afterward.

Exercise Library

Strong has a solid library. Covers everything most people will ever need, custom exercise creation works without friction.

Hevy similar story. Good library, easy to add custom movements.

Valen's library is built differently. Every exercise is mapped to its primary and secondary muscle groups at the subzone level, not just the broad muscle. Incline dumbbell press doesn't just say "chest." It loads upper chest primarily, mid chest secondarily, anterior deltoid as support. That mapping is what powers the recovery model, so it actually matters that it's accurate. You can also add custom exercises and assign them to specific muscle subzones manually.

Anterior Delt SECONDARY (30%)

Winner: Valen, because the library doing something useful with the data rather than just storing it.

Progress Tracking

Strong shows you charts of your lifts over time. Weight on the bar going up. Estimated 1RM trends. Clean, functional, does the job.

Hevy does this better visually. The charts are more readable, the volume breakdowns are more accessible, and the estimated 1RM graphs are genuinely satisfying to look at when things are going well.

Valen tracks E1RM per exercise, PRs, progressive overload streaks, and velocity. Velocity here means the rate at which your strength is moving, so you can see if you're progressing, stalling, or quietly regressing before it becomes obvious. You also get 7-day trend lines per muscle group so you can see training load patterns, not just raw numbers.

Squat e1RM Trend +12 lbs
Velocity: High Overload Streak: 3 Wks

Winner: Valen on depth. Hevy on presentation. Strong gets the job done if you just want the basics.

Recovery Tracking

Strong: does not do this.

Hevy: does not do this.

Valen: this is the whole point.

Every session gets processed through the Stimulus-Recovery-Adaptation model. Fatigue is calculated per muscle subzone, not per broad muscle group. It decays in real time based on training volume, intensity, and the muscle's individual recovery rate. You get a front and back body heatmap that updates hour by hour. Green means recovered and ready. Orange means partially recovered. Red means rest it.

Lateral Delt 100% Recovered
Front Delt Needs 24h

You can also manually flag soreness or fatigue on any muscle if the model doesn't match how you feel. The system adjusts. No wearable needed, no heart rate monitor, no sleep tracker. Just your workout log and a model that's been built carefully.

Winner: Valen by default since it's the only one playing this game.

Weekly Planning

Strong has routine templates. You build them, you follow them. It doesn't adapt to anything.

Hevy same thing. Good routine management, static planning.

Valen's weekly planner is dynamic. It takes your preferred split, your training frequency, and your real-time muscle fatigue state and suggests what to train each day. If you missed a session or trained something unexpectedly, it adjusts. It tells you which day is your peak readiness day for each muscle group so you can schedule your hardest sessions when your body is actually prepared for them. It also flags when your systemic fatigue is high enough that you should probably just rest regardless of what the plan says.

Winner: Valen. The other two don't have a weekly planner that knows anything about your body.

Muscle Balance and Volume Insights

Strong: not a feature.

Hevy: basic volume tracking by muscle group, nothing analytical.

Valen shows you anterior versus posterior volume balance, push versus pull ratios, and flags specialization penalties if you're hammering one muscle group while neglecting its counterpart. If your chest is getting 65% of your weekly volume and your back is getting 7%, Valen will quietly let you know you're building a posture problem. CNS strain is tracked separately from local muscle fatigue, so you can see when your nervous system is cooked even if individual muscles look recovered on the map.

Rest Timer & Plate Calculator

All three apps have functional rest timers and plate calculators. Valen gets a slight nod on the rest timer for being unobtrusive, but they all do the job fine. Moving on.

Winner: All the same.

Sharing and Social

Strong: no social feed. Some people consider this a feature.

Hevy: has a social feed where you can follow other users and see their workouts. Active community, some people love it, others find it completely irrelevant to why they downloaded a workout app.

Valen has share cards. You export your recovery heatmap overlaid on a photo as a card to post wherever you want. No in-app social feed, no followers, no community to maintain. Just a clean export if you want to show someone what your body looked like after a hard week.

Winner: depends entirely on whether you want a social feed in your workout app or not. If yes, Hevy. If no, Strong or Valen.

Offline Use & Pricing

Offline Use: Strong and Valen are fully offline first. Hevy works offline but syncs when connected. Valen gets a small nod for making offline the default rather than the fallback.

Free Tier: Hevy offers unlimited routines and full history. Valen offers full recovery heatmap, unlimited logging, exercise library with subzone mapping, all free. Valen wins on how much the free tier actually tells you about your body.

Pricing: Strong has a subscription tier for advanced features. Hevy is subscription based. Valen offers a free tier plus annual or lifetime Pro. Lifetime option exists, which is increasingly rare in the app world.

Who Should Use What

You want the fastest possible logging experience with zero distractions: Strong.

You want a free, modern logger with a social layer and clean progress visuals: Hevy.

You want to know what your body is actually doing between sessions and stop guessing what to train: Valen.

You want all three things: use Strong or Hevy for the logging speed if it matters that much to you, but Valen is the only one that answers the question every serious lifter eventually starts asking. Not "what did I lift" but "am I actually ready to lift again."

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Hevy or Strong track muscle recovery?

No. Neither Hevy nor Strong calculates recovery or fatigue. Both are logging and progress-tracking apps. Valen is the only one of the three with a recovery model, built on a Stimulus-Recovery-Adaptation system that tracks fatigue per muscle subzone.

What's free in Hevy, Strong, and Valen?

Hevy's free tier includes unlimited routines and full history. Strong's free tier is limited and pushes toward its subscription for advanced features. Valen's free tier includes the full recovery heatmap, unlimited logging, and the subzone-mapped exercise library, with 30 days of history.

Which app is fastest for logging sets?

Strong is built specifically around fast logging between rest periods and is the quickest of the three. Hevy is close behind. Valen logs just as completely, but every set also feeds the recovery model, which is the tradeoff for the extra data.

Do any of these apps track push/pull volume balance?

Hevy shows basic volume by muscle group with no analysis. Strong doesn't track this at all. Valen shows push versus pull ratio and anterior versus posterior balance directly, and flags specialization imbalances, for example, if chest is getting 65% of weekly volume while back gets 7%.
